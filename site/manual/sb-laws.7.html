sb-laws(7)

NAME

sb-laws — the platform's invariants, how each is checked, and where

LAW-31 lent access lasts one run

DESCRIPTION

A law is a statement the platform guarantees for every input, every tenant and every release. Laws are written here first, checked by tests, models, evals and production monitors, and tracked in the assurance ledger. A law violation in production is Sev-1, always.

Each law lists its checks:

Check Meaning
Model A TLA+ or Alloy model in proofs/ checks it over all small cases
Property A property-based test in laws/ checks it over generated cases
Fault A fault-injection test checks it under connector failures
Eval An eval suite measures it where the model's behaviour matters
Verify sb tenant verify checks it on a tenant's real data
Watch A production monitor alerts on it

LAW-01: Cite only what the asker can open

No answer cites, quotes or uses a chunk from a document that the asker can't open at answer time, according to the source's live ACL.

Checks: Model (Permissions.als: CitedImpliesLiveAllow) · Property, which asserts the source was asked, not just that the prefilter matched · Fault · Eval (permission) · Verify · Watch (sampled re-checks of citations).

LAW-02: No tenant crossing

No retrieval, query, cache or log read returns data from another tenant.

Checks: Model (NoCrossTenant) · Property with two seeded tenants on every CI run · adversarial queries, including hostile Host headers (SB-612) · Verify · Watch (ETENANT counter).

LAW-03: Unknown means refuse

An Unknown permission result (timeout, error, missing source, unmapped model) is treated exactly like Deny.

Checks: Model (UnknownNeverUsed) · Fault: full matrix through the mock connector · Verify (injects a timeout) · lint: exhaustive Decision switches.

LAW-04: Audit before act

Every tool call has a committed audit row before it executes. A consequential call also has a valid approval id in that row.

Checks: Model (Approvals.tla: AuditBeforeExec, ApprovalBeforeExec) · Property: audit replay reconstructs every call · Watch: audit-insert failures.

LAW-05: Ran means receipt

An action is reported as Ran only if the target returned a receipt. Otherwise it is DidNotRun.

Checks: Model (RanImpliesReceipt) · Fault: a mock with no receipt must yield DidNotRun · contract kit act/receipt-or-error.

LAW-06: Only signed, gated builds run

The build running in every cell is a signed release whose bundle passed the eval gate.

Checks: Model (Rollout.tla: OnlyGatedRuns) · deploy-time verification · Watch: hourly digest attestation per cell.

LAW-07: No secret in prompt, log or audit

No secret (credential, token, key) appears in any prompt, log line or audit row.

Checks: canary secrets in vault and fixtures · scanner over prompts, logs and audit in CI (tests/canary, SB-411) · laws: logs, prompts and audit rows, and secrets logged URL-escaped, base64 or hex, or under a credential-named attribute (SB-612) · Watch: the same scanner in production, findings are Sev-1 (sb-observability(7)).

LAW-08: At most one behind stable

No stable-channel tenant on the release train (custody factory or tenant) runs a release more than one behind the current stable. Detached cells are off the train (sb-factory(7)).

Checks: Model (Rollout.tla: OneBehindMax). Removing the promote guard produces a counterexample · release-controller check on every promotion · Watch: hourly lag.

LAW-09: Data is not instructions

Ingested content can't cause a tool call or exfiltrate data. Only the user's own request can lead to a tool call.

Checks: Eval (injection, zero tool calls and zero canary leaks) · Property: tool-argument provenance never from flagged spans · the pen test's corpus, security/attacks/injection.txt: every payload is flagged or stripped (SB-612).

LAW-10: Kill wins

Within 10 seconds of a kill switch being set, no new tool call or run starts in its scope.

Checks: Model (KillWins: once killed, no Executing transition) · timed test · drill every release.

LAW-11: Audit chain unbroken

The audit log is append-only. Every row's prev equals the previous row's hash, and every hourly anchor verifies.

Checks: tamper test · sb audit verify in CI, after restore drills and hourly in production.

LAW-12: Deprovision revokes fast

Within 5 minutes of a SCIM deactivate, no request from that user succeeds.

Checks: timed property test · Watch: requests by deactivated users.

LAW-13: The index is derived

Dropping and rebuilding a tenant's index from its sources yields the same set of (cite, ACL) pairs.

Checks: scheduled rebuild drill with set equality · Property on the ingest pipeline (idempotent per (cite, span)).

LAW-14: Every claim cited or labelled

Every claim in an answer or draft either cites an allowed chunk or carries the uncited label, set by code.

Checks: Property over the composer · Eval (citation) · Verify.

LAW-15: Approval binds one payload

An approval authorises exactly one payload, identified by its hash, approved by someone other than the requester. Any edit voids it.

Checks: Model (ExecutedMatchesApproved, NoSelfApproval) · Property: hash mismatch always refuses.

LAW-16: The manifest is the truth

A cell's live configuration equals its applied manifest. Every console change is a manifest diff.

Checks: Watch: hourly drift detector · Property: console actions produce manifest diffs.

LAW-17: Hand-off leaves no unlisted access

After a hand-off to tenant custody completes, the factory holds no access path to the cell (IAM role, deploy credential, break-glass role, network route, key wrap) except the grants listed in the hand-off record.

Checks: Model (Handoff.tla: NoUnlistedAccess) · Verify (sb factory access, which enumerates access paths from IaC state and IAM, sb-factory(1)) · Property: every hand-off run in the controller ends with only the retained grants (TestLAW17_HandoffLeavesOnlyRetainedGrants, red until SB-705) · Watch: monthly re-enumeration.

LAW-18: The root key always has a custodian

At every moment, at least one custodian can unwrap a cell's root key. A hand-off re-wraps under the new custodian before destroying the old wrap.

Checks: Model (Handoff.tla: KeyNeverOrphaned) · the wrap inventory's ordering guard: the factory wrap can't be destroyed unless every data key's tenant wrap unwraps now (TestLAW18_DestroyNeedsAWorkingWrap, SB-704) · step-ordering property over random hand-offs, aborts and failures in the controller (TestLAW18_HandoffNeverOrphansTheKey, red until SB-704 and SB-705) · Watch: key-wrap inventory per cell.

LAW-19: Spaces only narrow

No answer, citation, draft or action uses a chunk that the source's live ACL would deny, whatever any space policy says. A space can hide what someone could open, never show what they can't (ADR-0020, sb-spaces(7)).

Owner: platform lead. Checks: Model (Spaces.als: SpacesOnlyNarrow) · Property over random companies with spaces, labels, barriers and ACLs (TestLAW19_SpacesOnlyNarrow at the policy layer since SB-1004; TestLAW19_TheSpaceContextNeverWidens) · Static (make policy: only the base policy permits read, and the whole set allows nothing it doesn't) · Eval (space-leak, space-redteam).

LAW-20: Restricted stays home

Content labelled restricted reaches only members of a space that holds it, including in company-wide asks.

Owner: platform lead. Checks: Model (Spaces.als: RestrictedStaysHome) · Property (TestLAW20_RestrictedStaysHome on the ask path since SB-1006; TestLAW20_IngestNeverLabelsBelowRestricted) · Eval (space-leak, People, Finance and Executive cases; space-redteam).

LAW-21: Moves revoke fast

When the directory removes someone from a space's member groups, they lose that space within 5 minutes. A new membership grants nothing until the cell has confirmed it from the directory.

Owner: identity lead. Checks: Property on a fake clock (TestLAW21_MovesRevokeFast: SCIM moves through the request path, SB-1205) · Watch (move-to-revoke latency, W12).

LAW-22: Barriers hold

No answer, citation, draft or action crosses a declared information barrier, in either direction.

Owner: platform lead. Checks: Model (Spaces.als: BarriersHold) · Property (TestLAW22_BarriersHold: asks, drafts, tool calls and approvals, with source owners; SB-1203) · Eval (space-leak, space-redteam).

LAW-23: Grants are consented and revocable

A parent-org grant on a company space carries only the aggregates the company approved, never content, and stops within 5 minutes of being revoked (ADR-0014; built only if Q-01 is yes).

Owner: portfolio lead. Checks: Property (TestLAW23_GrantsAreConsentedAndRevocable, red until SB-1302).

LAW-24: The tree never widens

A space's place in the tree (sb-spaces(7), The tree) never lets anyone see, count or be told about a document they couldn't see from the space that holds it. A branch ask uses only what an ask in one of the branch's spaces the asker is a member of would use on its own; a team is never looser than its department, and what flows down to it never changes its members, label or what it holds; a department's roll-ups count only what its steward can open, and nothing but counts; a question that climbs the tree carries only itself, never a document.

Owner: platform lead. Checks: Property (TestLAW24_TheTreeNeverWidens: generated trees of departments and teams, with random members, labels, barriers and source ACLs, through branch asks, and the manifest's settings that flow down; SB-3907) · Selftest (TestSelf_LAW24CatchesAWideTree: a branch open to every team's members goes red).

LAW-25: A memory is its person's own

A memory (sb-ask(3), Memory I control) holds only what its person wrote, never text from a document they can open, and it is sent to the model only with that person's own asks to the ask agent: never with anyone else's ask, an agent's draft or an eval probe.

Owner: platform lead. Checks: Property (TestLAW25_AMemoryIsItsPersonsOwn: generated people with random memory lines, some pasted from documents they can open, through adds and asks by everyone; every model request is read for another person's line; SB-4303) · Selftest (TestSelf_LAW25CatchesASharedMemory: memory sent with every ask goes red).

LAW-26: A stored result is checked when read

A result the cell keeps and shows later (a research report, a meeting brief, and from W42 an inbox item or a routine's answer) shows the reader only cites they can open when it is shown, through every check a search makes; a claim whose every cite they have lost is not shown. What was true when it ran is no proof of what the reader may see now.

Owner: platform lead. Checks: Property (TestLAW26_AStoredResultIsCheckedWhenRead: generated reports over random documents and ACLs, with grants revoked between the run and the read; SB-4201) · Selftest (TestSelf_LAW26CatchesAStaleReport: a report shown as it was stored goes red).

LAW-27: A consultation is the asker's own

When a branch ask consults department agents (sb-ask(3), Ask each department), each consultation runs as the asker, in a team they are a member of, one level deep, and adds nothing the asker couldn't get by asking that team's agent directly: the same claims and cites. A team the asker isn't a member of is never consulted or named, at most 6 are consulted, and a consulted agent never consults again or calls a tool.

Owner: platform lead. Checks: Property (TestLAW27_AConsultationIsTheAskersOwn: generated departments with random teams, members, department agents, glossaries and source ACLs, through consulting branch asks compared with asking each team directly, and a consultation asked to consult; SB-4501) · Selftest (TestSelf_LAW27CatchesAWideConsultation: consulting every team, member or not, goes red).

LAW-28: An agent's writing says so

Anything an agent wrote says which agent and which version wrote it wherever it is shown: an answer, in the web app and in chat; a report or brief; a draft in a steward queue; and a note published from a draft, which keeps generated_by and approved_by in its front matter, so every cite of it, its export and its printout show the mark too (sb-agents(7), Marked as AI). The mark goes only when a person rewrites the text: a note published from a draft none of whose lines are left carries none.

Owner: platform lead. Checks: Property (TestLAW28_AnAgentsWritingSaysSo: generated answers, agent drafts and notes published from drafts unchanged, edited or rewritten, through the real answer, chat, draft and note paths and the index, each checked for the mark; SB-4706) · Selftest (TestSelf_LAW28CatchesAnUnmarkedNote: a note published without its mark goes red).

LAW-29: A rhythm only drafts, and stops with its subject

Every step a rhythm offers is a draft or a prompt that waits, unanswered, in the queue of the one person it names (its subject, its starter, the subject's manager) or of the space's stewards, and nothing a rhythm does publishes a note, sends a message or answers a step on its own. A rhythm stops at once when its subject leaves the space, its collection closes or goes, or its starter, its subject or a steward stops it: the next read of the space's rhythms shows it stopped, and no step is offered after that (sb-departments(7), Rhythms).

Owner: platform lead. Checks: Property (TestLAW29_ARhythmOnlyDraftsAndStopsWithItsSubject: random rhythms of up to 12 steps over up to 120 days, with random approvers, a clock that jumps, and the subject leaving at a random moment, through the real rhythms service, steward queues, folder connector and index; SB-4801) · Selftest (TestSelf_LAW29CatchesARhythmThatActsOrOutlivesItsSubject: a rhythm that ignores leaving, and one that answers its own steps, each go red).

LAW-30: A brief about a person draws only on what they published, and they can read it

A brief the platform makes about one person for someone else (the 1:1 prep a manager gets for a report) holds only what that person published: goals they own and their check-ins, and action items assigned to them, each line cited to something both the reader and the person can open. Nothing from the person's asks, inbox, memory, routines or unpublished drafts appears in it, no line rates or scores them, and the person gets the same text at the same time (sb-agents(7), 1:1 prep).

Owner: platform lead. Checks: Property (TestLAW30_ABriefAboutAPersonDrawsOnlyOnWhatTheyPublished: random published goals and action items, some in documents only one of the two can open, beside random private asks, memory entries and routines with unique markers, through the real rhythms service, folder connector and index; every brief line is published and openable by both, no private marker appears, and the two copies are identical; SB-4807) · Selftest (TestSelf_LAW30CatchesABriefThatLeaksAPrivateItem: a brief that adds the report's latest ask goes red).

LAW-31: Lent access lasts one run, names the lender, and its result reaches only people who can open every cite

When a steward's run needs a space they can't read, a person who can read it may lend it for one run (sb-agents(7), Borrow access for one run). The run reads that space as the lender, once; its draft names the lender; and a reader sees the draft only when they can open every one of its cites, checked live as them, so the loan widens what no one sees. A loan is answered once, yes or no, and never runs again: the next run asks again.

Owner: platform lead. Checks: Property (TestLAW31_LentAccessLastsOneRun: random lent drafts with random cites and random readers who can open part of them, through the real visibility gate, and a loan answered at random, through the real loan service and store, then answered again; SB-5303) · Selftest (TestSelfLentGateIsCaught: a gate that shows a lent draft to anyone, or to a reader who can open one of its cites, goes red).

External review (SB-612)

Before the platform scales past friendly tenants, an independent team pen-tests it against staging, with test tenants only. The scope and rules of engagement are in security/README.md. Every finding goes in the register, security/findings.yaml, with its severity, the laws it touches, and (once fixed) its BUG-nnnn entry. A fixed finding is also named in the threat model. make check fails while any critical or high finding isn't fixed, so a release can't go out over one. Medium and lower findings may be accepted with a reason in the threat model's residual risks. When a finding shows that a law was violated, a law test that would have caught it is added before the fix, and it must go red first.
